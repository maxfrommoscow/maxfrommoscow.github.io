Above the minimum: what Rushil's compact laminate test reports show

Every compliant compact laminate meets EN 438. The better question for a specifier is how much margin there is above it. Rushil's data sheets answer with figures — and in several properties the margin is large.

7 min read·Updated Oct 21, 2026

In short

  • Rushil's compact clears EN 438 by wide margins: bending strength of 136.1 MPa against 80, a modulus about 70 % above the floor, and 450 wear revolutions against the required 150.
  • In boiling water the panel gains 0.4 % mass and 0.2 % thickness — a fifth and a tenth of what the standard allows — and it took an 1800 mm ball drop with a 5 mm dent, where 1400 mm is required.
  • KLADS EDF is classified B-s1,d0 and sits well inside the limits: a fire growth rate of 16.15 W/s, about a seventh of the 120 allowed, and total heat release of 4.42 MJ against 7.5.

How this review was put together

Every figure here comes from Rushil Decor's published product data sheets for KLADS, TOPAZ and VAULT, which quote the EN 438-2 test report 272404/1, and from the fire classification data in the KLADS sheet. Each result is set against the requirement printed beside it in the same document. Unless stated otherwise, the values were measured on 6 mm exterior compact. The data sheets can be downloaded from this site, and the underlying test reports from Rushil's VIR website; both are listed at the end.

Why the margin matters

A standard sets a floor. A panel that clears it by a hair passes; so does one that clears it by a factor of three. The certificate reads the same — the data sheet does not. Margin is what a façade or a washroom draws on over decades of sun, water, cleaning agents and knocks, so it is worth reading the numbers rather than the word 'complies'.

Strength and stiffness

EN 438 asks for a bending strength of at least 80 MPa. Rushil's 6 mm exterior compact reaches 136.1 MPa along the panel and 98.8 MPa across it. The required modulus of elasticity is 9,000 MPa; the panel reaches 15,336 MPa along and 11,088 MPa across — about 70 % above the floor in the main direction. Tensile strength is twice the requirement: 120 MPa against 60.

The density of 1.47 g/cm³ sits above the 1.35 g/cm³ minimum, and a screw pull-out resistance of 3,514 N is reported to ISO 13894-1. For a fabricator these are the numbers that decide spans, deflection and fixing centres; our guide to specifying a compact worktop shows how.

Impact and wear

In the large-ball impact test the standard drops the ball from at least 1,400 mm and allows a dent below 10 mm. Rushil's panel took a drop of 1,800 mm and left a dent of 5 mm. Surface wear is where the margin is widest: EN 438 asks for at least 150 revolutions before the decor starts to wear through; the KLADS and VAULT data sheets report 450 — three times the requirement.

Water and dimensional stability

After immersion in boiling water EN 438 allows a mass gain of up to 2.0 % and a thickness gain of up to 2.0 %; Rushil's panel gained 0.4 % and 0.2 % — a fifth and a tenth of what is allowed. In water vapour the limit is 5.0 % mass gain; the result is 1.0 %. At elevated temperature the panel changed dimension by 0.10 % and 0.08 % against limits of 0.30 % and 0.60 %.

These are the properties that matter in a shower area, a commercial kitchen or a ventilated façade, where the panel lives with water for its whole life.

Sun and weather

For exterior grades EN 438 runs 3,000 hours of artificial weathering and asks for a grey-scale contrast of at least 3 and an appearance grade of at least 4. KLADS returned 4–5 and 5. In the 1,500-hour UV test the results were the same, and after climate-shock cycling the panel kept 98 % of its bending strength. The decor paper behind those results has a lightfastness above 5 and is covered by a UV film thicker than 50 µm, according to the data sheet.

Fire: well inside the class limits

KLADS EDF is classified B-s1,d0 to EN 13501-1 — the class the Welsh Government's Advice Note 22 gives as typical for fire-retardant HPL, and the highest it knows any HPL to reach. The data sheet shows how comfortably the 6 mm panel clears the limits behind that class in the single burning item test:

  • Fire growth rate (FIGRA): 16.15 W/s against a class B limit of 120 — about a seventh of what is allowed.
  • Total heat released in 600 seconds: 4.42 MJ against 7.5.
  • Smoke growth rate (SMOGRA): 0 against an s1 limit of 30.
  • Total smoke in 600 seconds: 29.11 m² against 50.
  • No lateral flame spread to the specimen edge and no flaming droplets.

Reading this against a specification

When comparing compact laminates, ask for the data sheet values, not only the statement that a product complies with EN 438. Rushil publishes its values together with the requirements and the test reports behind them, so each figure in this article can be checked at its source.

The same EN 438-2 report is quoted for KLADS exterior compact, TOPAZ worktops and VAULT cubicle panels; formats run to 1830 × 4330 mm, and fire-retardant grades are available in each range. Ask for the report that covers the grade and thickness you intend to order.

Common questions

Does EN 438 compliance mean all compact laminates perform the same?+−

No. EN 438 sets minimum values; each product reports its own results above them. Rushil's 6 mm exterior compact, for example, reports 136.1 MPa bending strength against a requirement of 80 and 450 wear revolutions against 150.

How far inside the B-s1,d0 limits is Rushil's fire-retardant exterior compact?+−

In the single burning item test the 6 mm panel showed a fire growth rate of 16.15 W/s against a limit of 120, total heat release of 4.42 MJ against 7.5, smoke growth of 0 against 30 and total smoke of 29.11 m² against 50.

Where can I check these figures?+−

In the KLADS, TOPAZ and VAULT data sheets, which can be downloaded from the documents section of this site, and in the test reports that Rushil publishes on its VIR website.
