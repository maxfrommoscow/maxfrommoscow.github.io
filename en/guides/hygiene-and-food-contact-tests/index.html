Antibacterial, antiviral, food-safe: what the laminate test reports actually say

'Antibacterial' and 'food-safe' are claims. Behind each one is a specific test with a specific result — and that is what belongs in a specification.

7 min read·Updated Oct 19, 2026

In short

  • Rushil's compact surfaces reduce bacteria by more than 99.99 % (JIS Z 2801) and viruses by more than 99.99 % (ISO 21702); 99 % is the threshold for antibacterial activity.
  • For food contact, TOPAZ stays within the EN 1186 overall migration limit of 10 mg/dm² in all three tests, and formaldehyde migration is well under the 15 mg/kg limit.
  • These are laboratory results on the panel surface: they support regular cleaning rather than replace it, and an EU buyer should also ask for a Declaration of Compliance under Regulation 10/2011.

Antibacterial: JIS Z 2801

JIS Z 2801 inoculates the surface with a defined quantity of bacteria, holds it at body-like temperature and humidity for 24 hours, and counts what survives compared with an untreated reference. The result is a reduction: the method treats 99 % as the threshold for antibacterial activity.

The compact grades return a reduction above 99.99 %. That means bacteria deposited on the surface do not multiply on it between cleans. It does not mean the surface cleans itself, and it is not a substitute for cleaning.

Antiviral and antifungal: ISO 21702 and ASTM G21

ISO 21702 applies the same logic to viruses on non-porous surfaces, measuring the reduction in infectious virus after a defined contact time. The result here is also above 99.99 %.

ASTM G21 exposes the material to a mix of fungal spores in warm, humid conditions for 28 days and rates the growth from 0 to 4. The compact grades are rated 0, no growth, where 1 is accepted — relevant for washrooms, changing rooms and anywhere surfaces stay damp.

Food contact: migration, not marketing

Under EU Regulation 10/2011 the question for a food-contact surface is what migrates from it into food. EN 1186 measures overall migration — everything that leaves the surface — against a limit of 10 mg/dm². The results are 9.06, 3.07 and not detected across three successive tests, which is how a surface behaves in repeated use.

EN 13130 measures specific substances one at a time. The TOPAZ data sheet reports melamine, bisphenol A, phenol, phthalates, formaldehyde and primary aromatic amines all as not detected. Rushil's updated food-contact summary of June 2026 quotes 3 mg/kg for formaldehyde and 0.5 mg/kg for melamine, but the SGS laboratory reports it links to show that these are the laboratory's reporting limits. Melamine was not detected in any of three successive migrations, against a limit of 2.5 mg/kg; formaldehyde measured 5.31 and 4.29 mg/kg in the first two migrations and was not detected in the third, against a limit of 15 mg/kg. All are passes.

Two further checks close the gaps the chemistry leaves. Sensory examination to DIN 10955 scored 0 for both odour and taste transfer, against a permissible 2.5, and the data sheet reports a screen against the 253 substances on the REACH candidate list of substances of very high concern. The updated summary also reports that the laminate meets NSF/ANSI 35, the North American standard for food-service work surfaces.

What none of these tests prove

Every result above was measured on test samples in a laboratory. It describes the material, not a finished installation: a badly sealed joint or a damaged edge in a kitchen is a hygiene problem no surface test can rule out.

Test coverage also matters. Testing is carried out on particular decors and finishes, and a range is not automatically covered end to end — ask whether the decor and finish you are specifying are within the tested scope. For food contact, the document an EU buyer needs is a Declaration of Compliance under Regulation 10/2011; ask for it alongside the reports.

And wording matters. In the EU, describing a product as antibacterial can bring it under the rules for treated articles in the Biocidal Products Regulation. Quote the test and the result, as this article does, rather than a bare claim.

Common questions

Is compact laminate antibacterial?+−

The surface reduces bacteria by more than 99.99 % in the JIS Z 2801 test, where 99 % is the threshold for antibacterial activity. That is a laboratory result on the panel surface; it supports, and does not replace, regular cleaning.

Can compact laminate be used for food preparation?+−

The TOPAZ grade is tested for food contact to EN 1186 and EN 13130 and for odour and taste transfer to DIN 10955. Confirm that the decor and finish you specify are within the tested scope, and ask for a Declaration of Compliance under Regulation 10/2011.

What did the formaldehyde migration test find?+−

In SGS tests to EN 13130-1, formaldehyde measured 5.31 and 4.29 mg/kg in the first two of three successive migrations and was not detected in the third, against a limit of 15 mg/kg. The TOPAZ data sheet reports the third result as not detected; the 3 mg/kg in Rushil's food-contact summary is the laboratory's reporting limit. The reports are available on request.
