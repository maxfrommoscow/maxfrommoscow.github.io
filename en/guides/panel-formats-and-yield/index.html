Panel formats and yield: why 4330 × 1830 changes a cut list

Format is usually treated as a logistics detail. On a large cut list it is the difference between a viable price and an uncompetitive one.

5 min read·Updated Aug 13, 2026

In short

  • One 4330 × 1830 mm panel covers 7.92 m², more than two and a half 2440 × 1220 mm panels, so long runs need fewer joints and an elevation fewer panels.
  • Yield decides the price of a cut list: a 1300 mm part fits three times along a 4330 mm sheet but only twice along a 3050 mm one.
  • The compact programme runs from 2440 × 1220 to 4330 × 1830 mm, with 3050 × 1300 and 3660 × 1830 mm in between; confirm availability per product and grade.

Three things format decides

Panel format looks like a shipping question and is actually a design and cost question. It decides where joints fall, how much material becomes offcut, and whether a decor reads as continuous across an elevation.

A single 4330 × 1830 mm panel covers 7.92 m². A 2440 × 1220 mm panel covers 2.98 m². Three of the smaller panels give slightly more area than one large one, but they also give two extra joints, two extra edge treatments and a cut list with more constrained nesting.

Yield is where the money is

On a repeated cut list — a shopfitting rollout, a run of hotel bathrooms, a façade with a regular grid — the pieces are a fixed size and the question is how efficiently they nest into the sheet.

A part that is 1300 mm long fits three times along a 4330 mm sheet with 430 mm left over, or twice along a 3050 mm sheet with 450 mm left over. Which sheet is cheaper per square metre is irrelevant next to which one produces less waste for that specific part. This is why fabricators ask for the format list before quoting, and why a specification that fixes a format without checking the cut list often costs more than one that does not.

Joints as a design decision

Every joint is a line in the finished work. On a worktop run it is visible from above at close range; on an elevation it is part of the composition whether or not anyone designed it.

Choosing the format from the longest uninterrupted run means joints land where they were intended. Choosing the run from the format available means the joints land wherever the arithmetic puts them. The first approach costs nothing extra at design stage and is close to impossible to retrofit later.

Decor continuity across a batch

Larger formats reduce the number of panels in an elevation, which reduces the number of opportunities for a shade difference to show. Decorative papers are produced in batches, and while a range engineered for shade consistency holds tolerance well across thicknesses and production runs, fewer panels is still fewer joins at which a difference could be visible.

For a project delivered in phases, ordering the full quantity at once and storing it is usually a better answer than matching a second batch to a first.

The formats to work from

The compact programme runs from 2440 × 1220 mm at 2.98 m² up to 4330 × 1830 mm at 7.92 m², with 3050 × 1300 mm (3.97 m²), 3660 × 1830 mm (6.70 m²) and several intermediate sizes in between. The Materix collections are built around maximum widths of 1300 and 1830 mm respectively, which is what makes them a specification tool rather than a shade card.

Areas on this site are calculated from the dimensions rather than transcribed, so a format list and its square-metre figures cannot drift apart.

Common questions

What is the largest panel available?+−

4330 × 1830 mm, covering 7.92 m² in a single panel. Availability by product and grade should be confirmed for a specific order.

How do I calculate how many sheets I need?+−

Divide the required area by the panel area only as a first approximation — it ignores nesting. For a real figure, give the fabricator the part list and let them nest it. The quotation form on this site calculates area from sheet quantity and panel size, which is the input a quotation needs.

Are large formats more expensive per square metre?+−

This site publishes no prices, so the honest answer is to request a quotation. What is worth carrying into that conversation is that per-square-metre cost and delivered cost diverge once offcut, joints and edge treatments are counted.
