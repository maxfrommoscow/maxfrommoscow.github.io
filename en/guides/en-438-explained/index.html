EN 438 explained: the standard behind every laminate specification

Every credible laminate data sheet is written against EN 438. Knowing how its parts divide up makes the sheets comparable.

5 min read·Updated Aug 13, 2026

In short

  • EN 438 is the standard behind every credible laminate data sheet. Part 2 holds the test methods, Part 3 covers thin laminates bonded to a board, Part 4 self-supporting compact.
  • Read every row as method, requirement and result: in the EN 438-2.10 wear test Rushil's compact must reach 150 revolutions and achieves 450.
  • Appearance grade 5 means no visible change. EN 438 does not cover fire (EN 13501-1), emissions (EN 16516) or food contact (EN 1186 and EN 13130).

The parts, and which one applies to you

EN 438 is a multi-part standard, and the part number tells you what kind of product you are looking at.

  • Part 2 — the test methods. Everything else refers back to it. When a data sheet cites EN 438-2.10 it is naming a specific test procedure.
  • Part 3 — classification and requirements for thin laminates, under 2 mm. Bonded to a substrate. Grades HGS and VGS.
  • Part 4 — classification and requirements for compact laminate, interior. Self-supporting. Grades CGS and CGF.
  • Part 6 — classification and requirements for exterior-grade compact. Weathering requirements Part 4 does not contain.

Reading a test row properly

A row in a laminate test table has three parts, and a value quoted without the other two is not information.

The method says what was done — EN 438-2.10, resistance to surface wear, is an abrasive wheel run against the surface until the decor breaks through at a defined point. The requirement is the threshold the standard sets, here at least 150 revolutions. The observed value is what the sample achieved, here 450.

Take the 450 out of that context and it means nothing. Presented with its method and requirement, it says the product clears the standard by a factor of three.

Grades within a classification

Within each part, grade codes narrow things further. HGS and VGS are horizontal and vertical general-purpose thin laminates — the horizontal grade is built for surfaces that get used, the vertical for surfaces that are mostly looked at.

For compact, CGS is the general-purpose grade and CGF the fire-retardant one. For exterior, the codes describe the grade and the UV configuration together. The grade is not a marketing tier: it changes which test results apply to the panel in front of you.

Appearance grades, and why 5 is not a percentage

Many EN 438 results are reported as an appearance grade from 1 to 5 rather than a measurement. The scale is defined: grade 5 is no visible change; grade 4 is a slight change of gloss or colour visible only at certain angles; grade 3 is a moderate change; grade 2 is marked; grade 1 is blistering or delamination.

So a result of 5 against a requirement of 4 means the sample showed no visible change where the standard would have accepted a slight one. It is a defined observation, not a score out of five.

What EN 438 does not cover

Fire classification is EN 13501-1. Formaldehyde and VOC emissions are EN 16516. Food-contact migration is EN 1186 and EN 13130. Antimicrobial performance is JIS Z 2801, ISO 21702 and ASTM G21.

A complete laminate specification therefore cites several standards, and each one covers a different question. A data sheet that answers everything with EN 438 has not addressed fire, emissions or food contact at all.

Common questions

What is the difference between EN 438-3 and EN 438-4?+−

Part 3 classifies thin laminates that are bonded to a substrate. Part 4 classifies compact laminates that are self-supporting. The part number on a data sheet is the quickest way to tell which kind of product it describes.

What does HGS mean on a laminate data sheet?+−

Horizontal General Purpose Standard — a thin laminate grade under EN 438-3 intended for horizontal surfaces. VGS is the vertical equivalent, for surfaces subject to less wear.

Does EN 438 cover fire performance?+−

No. Reaction to fire is classified under EN 13501-1, using tests including EN 13823 and EN ISO 11925-2. EN 438 covers mechanical, surface and dimensional performance.
