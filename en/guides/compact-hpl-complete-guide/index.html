Compact HPL: a complete guide to panels, grades and uses

Compact HPL is laminate all the way through: a finished, self-supporting panel that needs no substrate, with a dense core that can be machined and left on view. This guide explains how to choose the grade, plan the panel and finish the details.

6 min read·Updated Nov 26, 2026

In short

  • Compact HPL is pressed from many layers of resin-impregnated paper into a solid panel. The ICDLI describes compact from 6 mm as self-supporting, which is why it can stand alone as a cubicle door, worktop or façade panel.
  • EN 438-4 covers interior compact in two types, standard CGS and fire-retardant CGF; EN 438-6 covers exterior compact, with EDS and EDF for severe outdoor conditions. The grade tells you which product to start from; the project's fire strategy decides the evidence.
  • Rushil's VAULT and TOPAZ come in 10 to 18 mm and sheets up to 1830 × 4330 mm, and reach 450 revolutions to initial wear where 150 are required. These are test results, not a declaration of conformity.

How this guide was put together

This guide draws on the two European product standards for compact laminate, EN 438-4 for interior and EN 438-6 for exterior grades, read in their 2016 editions; on two ICDLI leaflets, on the characteristics of HPL (December 2017) and on processing compact laminates (June 2017); and on Rushil's data sheets for VAULT, TOPAZ and KLADS.

What compact HPL is

Compact HPL is made the same way as decorative HPL, from layers of kraft paper impregnated with phenolic resin under a melamine-impregnated decor paper, pressed together at high pressure and temperature. The difference is the number of layers. A thin decorative sheet under 2 mm has to be bonded to a board; compact is pressed thick enough to be the panel itself, with a decor on one or both faces.

The ICDLI describes compact laminates of 6 mm and more as self-supporting, while thinner HPL, particularly up to 2 mm, needs a substrate. That changes how a project is built. A washroom partition, a locker door, a slim worktop or a rainscreen panel can be cut, drilled and installed as one piece, with an edge that is laminate all the way through and needs no edge band.

Read the grade before choosing the panel

For interior compact, EN 438-4 uses a three-letter code. In CGS, C stands for compact, G for general purpose and S for standard grade. CGF is similar to CGS but with improved fire retardance, and also meets the special requirements of specified tests, which the standard notes may vary with the application and the country. A grade label points to the right product; the classification a project needs comes from its fire strategy.

Exterior compact is covered by EN 438-6, which adds resistance to light and weathering. Its grades EGS and EGF are intended for moderate outdoor conditions, and EDS and EDF for severe ones, with long-term exposure to strong sunlight and weather. Rushil's KLADS is made in the EDS and EDF grades, with a UV-protective film, and its 6 mm EDF panel reached B-s1,d0 to EN 13501-1. That is a test result, not a declaration of conformity; the façade as built is classified as a system.

Match thickness and format to the design

Thickness follows the job: the span between supports, the panel size, the hardware and the loads. The ICDLI notes that sheets over 8 mm suit large horizontal surfaces with more widely spaced supports, so a self-supporting worktop or partition needs no carrier board underneath. Rushil makes VAULT, for cubicles, partitions and lockers, and TOPAZ, for kitchen tops and counters, in 10 to 18 mm, and KLADS for façades in 6 to 12 mm.

Sheet size changes the look and the cut list. VAULT and TOPAZ come in 1830 × 1830, 1830 × 2165, 1830 × 3660 and 1830 × 4330 mm, so a full-height partition or a long counter can often be cut from one sheet without a joint. Check transport, access and handling for the largest format, and map joints and access panels before ordering. The guide to panel formats and yield on this site shows how the sheet size changes a cut list.

Plan machining and fixing before delivery

The ICDLI's processing guide asks for compact sheets to be stored flat and level and machined with carbide or diamond tooling. Compact moves a little with the climate, so every fixing needs room for expansion and contraction, and panels on a substructure need a stable, aligned support. Plan the fixed and sliding points with the installer rather than treating compact as a rigid board.

Show exposed edges, corners, holes, cut-outs and access panels on the drawings. For blind holes drilled into the face, the ICDLI asks for at least 1.5 mm of sheet to remain; for holes parallel to the face, at least 3 mm on each side. Expansion-anchor inserts give the highest screw pull-out strength, and Rushil's VAULT reached 3514 N in the ISO 13894-1 screw pull-out test. The guide to machining compact HPL on this site covers sawing, milling and drilling in detail.

Keep the finished surface easy to maintain

Compact needs no special care: the ICDLI refers users to its normal HPL cleaning guidance, which starts with water and a soft cloth. The closed surface and the dense core are why compact is chosen for washrooms, changing rooms, laboratories and kitchens. Show the installer how to remove the protective film, and include joints, cut edges and hardware in the building's cleaning plan.

Rushil's VAULT shows what this looks like in figures: 0.4 % mass gain after immersion in boiling water against 2 % allowed, grade 5 for staining, more than 99.99 % bacterial reduction under JIS Z 2801 and formaldehyde emission of 0.010 mg/m³ under EN 16516. These are test results, not a declaration of conformity. The guide to cleaning HPL surfaces on this site gives the day-to-day routine.

Common questions

What is compact HPL used for?+−

As a finished, self-supporting panel: washroom cubicles, partitions, lockers, worktops, laboratory benches, wall panels and exterior cladding. Choose the grade for the location, then design the supports and fixings.

What is the difference between CGS and CGF?+−

EN 438-4 describes CGS as standard-grade interior compact. CGF is similar but has improved fire retardance and meets additional specified tests, which can vary with the application and the country.

What thickness of compact HPL should I choose?+−

There is no single thickness for every use. Choose it from the span, loads and hardware, and check the product data. Rushil's interior compact comes in 10 to 18 mm.

Can compact HPL be used outside?+−

Yes, in an exterior grade under EN 438-6. EDS and EDF grades are intended for severe outdoor conditions; Rushil's KLADS is made in both, with a UV-protective film. Assess the complete façade, including cavity, subframe and fixings.
