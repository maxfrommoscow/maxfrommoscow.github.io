Installing a 12 mm compact worktop: supports, undermount sinks, hobs and joints

A 12 mm compact top gives a kitchen or washroom the slimmest edge there is, with a core that can be left exposed. It looks effortless when the work under it has been planned: supports, sink fixing, cut-outs and joints.

6 min read·Updated Nov 26, 2026

In short

  • Compact laminate over 8 mm is suited to large horizontal surfaces with more widely spaced supports, the ICDLI says, so a 12 mm top needs no chipboard underneath. The exact spacing comes from the panel maker for the load and layout.
  • Drill blind holes so that at least 1.5 mm of panel remains, do not use tongue-and-groove joints in compact under 10 mm, and round every internal corner of a cut-out to at least 6 mm.
  • Rushil's TOPAZ is a compact made for worktops, from 10 to 18 mm thick, with grade 5 in the 160 °C dry-heat test and 450 revolutions to initial wear where 150 are required. These are test results, not a declaration of conformity.

How this guide was put together

This guide follows the ICDLI's June 2017 leaflet on processing compact laminates, which covers supports, drilling and joints, and two proHPL publications: the July 2016 guide to worktops with HPL surfaces, for sinks, hobs and cut-outs, and the January 2022 general processing recommendations. Rushil's TOPAZ data sheet supplies the surface test results.

Plan the supports before the cabinets

The ICDLI explains how compact behaves by thickness: under 3 mm it is normally bonded to a rigid support, up to 5 mm it needs closely spaced supports, and above that it is usually self-supporting. Sheets over 8 mm are suitable for large horizontal surfaces with more widely spaced supports. A 12 mm top is well inside that range, which is why it can run over cabinets without a chipboard carrier.

Draw the support plan first: cabinet sides, front and back rails, open bays for a dishwasher or oven, joints and overhangs. Then lay every cut-out over it. The span to check is the clear distance under each remaining strip of material, which near a hob or a sink can be far shorter than a cabinet width suggests. Ask the panel maker for the permitted span and overhang for the load, and show the rails on the worktop template.

Fix the undermount sink into the panel

A flush or undermount sink is where a compact top shows its strength: the cut edge is laminate all the way through, so it can be profiled, polished and left on view with no edge band to lift. Place the sink template and its fixing system on the real panel section and check the room needed by the bowl, overflow, tap and clamps, and how the weight of a full sink reaches the cabinets or a carrier.

Clips are usually screwed into blind holes in the underside. The ICDLI asks for the drilling depth to leave at least 1.5 mm of sheet thickness, and for holes drilled parallel to the face at least 3 mm on each side. Expansion-anchor inserts give the highest screw pull-out strength. Seal the rim with a bead suited to the sink, and keep the clips reachable so the sink can be serviced later.

Cut the hob opening with round corners

Use the template of the exact appliance and draw the opening, the corner radii and the supports together with the hob body beneath. Look hard at the narrow strips left in front of and behind the opening: proHPL asks for a rigid base under the worktop during sawing, routing and drilling so that these strips cannot break or splinter.

Every internal corner must be rounded. proHPL's worktop guide sets a minimum radius of 6 mm for cut-outs up to 250 mm side length, and warns that sharp corners lead to cracks, especially around hobs, where repeated heating increases shrinkage stress. Use a router rather than a jigsaw where possible, keep every cut edge free of chips, and follow the appliance maker's instructions for clearances and ventilation.

Place the joint where it can be supported

Mark the seams on the cabinet plan with their clamps or connectors drawn in section. A connector designed for a deep chipboard top may need more depth than a 12 mm panel can give, so choose hardware made for thin compact and make sure the installer can reach it from below. Bring the cabinets to level before pulling the joint together.

The ICDLI rules out tongue-and-groove joints in compact sheets under 10 mm; above that, groove width and the remaining walls should each be at least 3 mm and the groove no deeper than 10 mm. Leave room for the panel to expand and contract, as the ICDLI asks for every compact installation. Keep the seam away from sinks and appliance bays, and seal it so it stays hygienic.

Template the vanity like the kitchen

A bathroom top looks simpler, but a large basin, tap holes and an open cabinet can remove support exactly where people lean on it. Take the template once cabinets and wall finishes are fixed, and mark the finished edge, tap clearance, wall junction and any removable plumbing panel. Use the basin maker's fixing and support instructions with the same rules for blind holes and corner radii.

Before handover, look at the visible core around every cut-out, check the perimeter seals and test the plumbing. Show the owner where the loads are supported and how to reach the clips, and keep the template and the appliance or basin references. A future replacement can then be cut to the same opening without guesswork.

Common questions

What support spacing does a 12 mm compact worktop need?+−

The ICDLI says compact over 8 mm is suited to horizontal use with more widely spaced supports, but gives no single figure. Ask the panel maker for the span and overhang for your load and layout, and check the narrow strips around cut-outs.

Can I undermount a sink in a 12 mm compact top?+−

Yes, with a fixing system made for compact. Drill the clip holes so that at least 1.5 mm of panel remains behind them, and show how the full sink is carried.

Does a 160 °C heat test mean the hob needs no clearance?+−

No. The dry-heat test describes the surface. Clearances, ventilation and the cut-out size come from the appliance instructions, and the corners need a radius of at least 6 mm.

Can I join thin compact with tongue and groove?+−

Not under 10 mm, according to the ICDLI. At 12 mm it is possible with grooves no deeper than 10 mm and walls of at least 3 mm, but connectors made for thin compact are often simpler.
