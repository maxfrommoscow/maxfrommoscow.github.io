Laminate surfaces and formaldehyde: what emission research measured

Most of the formaldehyde in furniture comes from the board inside, not the surface outside. Emission researchers have measured how much a laminate surface holds back, and where it lets go.

6 min read·Updated Oct 22, 2026

In short

  • Most of the formaldehyde in furniture comes from the board inside; in a 2025 study a melamine-impregnated paper facing cut particleboard's total volatile emissions by 93 %.
  • The edges decide: melamine laminate cut formaldehyde emission by 4.4 % with open edges and by 37.4 % with all edges sealed.
  • Compact contains no wood-based board, the usual source. Rushil's VAULT reports 0.010 mg/m³ to EN 16516, against the EU limit of 0.062 mg/m³ — a test result, not a declaration of conformity.

How this review was put together

This article summarises six peer-reviewed studies: from Soongsil University (Kim, Journal of Hazardous Materials, 2009), Kyungpook National University (Park et al., Journal of Adhesion Science and Technology, 2013; Park et al., Journal of the Korean Wood Science and Technology, 2016), the Chinese Academy of Forestry (Zhu et al., Polymers, 2025), the California Department of Public Health (Chen et al., Building and Environment, 2018) and the Beijing Institute of Technology with the same Californian team (He et al., Environment International, 2019). They concern wood-based boards faced with melamine-impregnated paper, films and coatings, the closest relatives of HPL-faced boards; we found no peer-reviewed chamber study on HPL-faced boards specifically, and say so.

The board is the source

Particleboard and MDF are bonded with formaldehyde-based resins, and the board's own grade dominates what a finished panel emits. In the Kyungpook chamber study, the formaldehyde emission grade of the board strongly affected the emission of the panel whatever laminate was put on it. The surface can only hold back what the board releases.

A melamine skin as a lid

Every surface overlay tested in that study, low-pressure melamine laminate, PVC film and urethane-coated paper, dramatically reduced formaldehyde emission compared with the bare board, and the overlays also cut emissions of total volatile organic compounds and toluene. The two coatings did the opposite for volatiles: a direct coating released mostly styrene, a UV coating mostly toluene. Kim at Soongsil found the same pattern in laminate flooring: emissions fell at the step where melamine-impregnated paper was pressed onto the board.

A 2025 study from the Chinese Academy of Forestry measured particleboard in a 1 m³ chamber before and after decoration. Melamine-impregnated paper, the least air-permeable of the three facings, cut total volatile emissions by 93 % and introduced no new odorous substances. Polypropylene film cut them by 83 % but introduced xylene; PVC film raised them by 67 % and brought in 12 new compounds.

Edges are where it escapes

The Kyungpook team then sealed or left open the edges of laminated particleboard. With open edges, the melamine laminate reduced formaldehyde emission by only 4.4 % compared with the bare board; with all edges sealed, by 37.4 %. A polypropylene film went from 33.7 % to 80.7 %. A coated MDF with open edges even emitted 5.3 % more than the control, from the coating's solvent.

In California, researchers kept two laminate floorings in 20-litre chambers for six months, with and without exposed click-joint seams and cut edges. Seams and edges contributed significantly to emissions, especially for the product with a high-emitting core, and the effect could last long after installation. A follow-up model, fitted to a year and a half of chamber data from exposed edges and seams, showed that long-term emission includes formaldehyde newly formed by hydrolysis of the resin, not only what was there at the start.

What this means for HPL and compact

For decorative HPL on a board, the research points one way: the laminate is a lid, and lids leak at the rim. Seal every edge, cut-out and drilled hole, choose a low-emitting board, and avoid finishes that add volatiles of their own. Compact HPL contains no wood-based board at all; its paper and resin are cured into one solid sheet. Rushil's VAULT data sheet reports formaldehyde emission of 0.010 mg/m³ to EN 16516, against the EU limit of 0.062 mg/m³ for furniture and wood-based articles that applies from August 2026; that is a test result, not a declaration of conformity, as a separate article on this site explains.

Common questions

Does a laminate surface reduce formaldehyde from the board?+−

Yes, in the studies reviewed here: every overlay tested, including low-pressure melamine laminate, dramatically reduced formaldehyde emission compared with the bare board, and melamine-impregnated paper cut total volatile emissions by 93 %. How much it helps depends heavily on the edges.

Why do the edges of a laminated panel matter so much?+−

Because emissions escape where the surface stops. In one study, melamine laminate on particleboard cut formaldehyde emission by 4.4 % with open edges and by 37.4 % with sealed edges; in another, seams and cut edges of laminate flooring contributed significantly to emissions for months.

Does compact HPL emit formaldehyde?+−

Compact contains no wood-based board, the usual source. Rushil's VAULT data sheet reports 0.010 mg/m³ to EN 16516, against the EU limit of 0.062 mg/m³ for furniture and wood-based articles from August 2026. That is a test result, not a declaration of conformity.
