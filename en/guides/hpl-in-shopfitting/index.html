HPL and compact in shopfitting: counters, displays and fitting rooms

A shop interior is refitted more often than almost any other, and in between it takes trolleys, bags, sliding goods and daily cleaning. HPL, used in building and furniture for many decades, handles both: it goes in fast and it lasts. Here is which product goes where, how the German industry group proHPL recommends working it, and what Rushil's surfaces take.

6 min read·Updated Nov 19, 2026

In short

  • HPL surfaces are hard and resistant to abrasion, impact and scratching, largely withstand vandalism and are easy to clean, says the German industry group proHPL, and bonded panels and compact allow fast, dry installation on site.
  • Use the horizontal grade HGS on counters and tops, the vertical grade VGS on low-wear walls and displays, compact where edges are exposed or wet, and long sheets up to 4330 mm to avoid joints in elevations.
  • Condition HPL and board together for at least three days, build symmetrically with the same production direction on both faces and press below 60 °C. Rushil's Decorative HPL takes more than 400 wear revolutions where 150 are required.

How this guide was put together

This guide follows the general processing recommendations published by proHPL, the HPL group of the German plastics industry association pro-K, in January 2022, and Rushil's data sheets for Decorative HPL, TOPAZ and VAULT. We found no surface standard written for shopfitting alone, so the guide works from the properties EN 438 tests and from how fabricators are advised to handle the material.

Why shops use HPL

proHPL lists furniture and interior fit-out among the typical uses of HPL, and its summary reads like a shopfitter's brief. The surfaces are hard and resistant to abrasion, impact and scratching; they largely withstand vandalism, are easy to clean and last a long time. HPL comes in a wide range of colours, decors and textures, and both bonded panels and compact allow fast, easy installation on site.

Refits benefit most. In a dry refurbishment, proHPL notes, existing wallpaper, textile coverings or tiles need not be removed before panels go on. For a shop that has to reopen on Monday, that is often the deciding argument.

Which product goes where

Counters, cash desks and table tops take sliding goods, keys and coins, so they need the horizontal general-purpose grade, HGS, bonded to a board. Wall panels, displays and furniture sides see less wear and can use the vertical grade, VGS. Rushil's Decorative HPL is made from 0.5 to 1.8 mm in sheets up to 1300 × 3050 mm, in four gloss levels from high gloss to matt.

Where an edge is exposed or gets wet, as on a café counter, a fitting-room partition or a staff washroom, self-supporting compact needs no board and no edge banding: TOPAZ for worktops and counters, VAULT for partitions and lockers. For long elevations and back walls, the JUMBO formats run to 1830 × 4330 mm, so a wall of shelving or a counter front can be clad with far fewer joints.

Getting the board right

A bonded panel is only as good as its build. proHPL asks for a board that is dimensionally stable with a smooth surface, from chipboard, MDF or plywood to honeycomb, foam or mineral boards where weight, moisture or fire call for them. HPL and board should be conditioned together at about 18 to 25 °C and 50 to 65 % relative humidity: air around every sheet for at least ten days, and at least three days stacked as they will be bonded.

Build symmetrically. HPL on both faces, or a balancing sheet offered by the HPL maker, with the same production direction front and back, because HPL moves differently along and across the sheet. Spread the adhesive evenly and equally on both sides, choose its durability class to suit the moisture, D3 or D4 near water, and press at around 20 °C for a stress-free panel and not above 60 °C.

Cutting, edges and cut-outs

The melamine surface is hard, so proHPL recommends carbide tooling, and diamond for series work. On a table or panel saw, cut with the decor side up; with a hand-held circular saw or jigsaw, cut from the back to keep the visible edge clean. Round every internal corner of a cut-out to a radius of at least 6 mm, because a sharp corner can start a crack.

Compact edges are the same material as the face, so they are machined, chamfered and left exposed without banding. That makes rounded counter ends, slots for display fittings and routed handles straightforward on a CNC machine.

What Rushil's surfaces take

Rushil's Decorative HPL exceeds 400 revolutions in the EN 438 wear test, where more than 150 is required. It takes more than 33 N in the small-ball impact test against 20 N required and a drop height above 1800 mm in the large-ball test against 800 mm, and it is graded 5, no visible change, in staining groups 1 and 2.

TOPAZ and VAULT compact reach 450 revolutions in the wear test and grade 5 for scratching, the top of the scale. For a counter that is wiped many times a day, those margins mean a surface that still looks right at the next refit. These are test results, not a declaration of conformity.

A shopfitting checklist

Put these in the drawing and the order.

  • HGS on counters and tops; VGS only on low-wear vertical surfaces.
  • Compact where edges are exposed or wet; JUMBO formats for long elevations.
  • Board type, balancing sheet and production direction for every bonded panel.
  • Adhesive durability class D3 or D4 near water; press temperature below 60 °C.
  • Internal corners of cut-outs rounded to at least 6 mm.
  • Decor, finish and sheet format confirmed against a current sample.

Common questions

Which HPL is best for a shop counter?+−

The horizontal general-purpose grade, HGS, bonded to a stable board, or self-supporting compact such as TOPAZ where the edges are exposed or the counter gets wet. Rushil's Decorative HPL exceeds 400 revolutions in the EN 438 wear test, more than 150 being required.

How do I stop HPL panels from warping?+−

Condition HPL and board together, build the panel symmetrically with HPL or a balancing sheet on both faces in the same production direction, apply the same amount of adhesive on both sides and press at moderate temperature, not above 60 °C, as proHPL recommends.

Can HPL be fixed over old tiles in a shop refit?+−

proHPL notes that in a dry refurbishment existing wallpaper, textile coverings or tiles need not be removed before HPL panels are installed. The substrate still has to be sound and the fixing system suited to it.

What is the largest HPL sheet for shopfitting?+−

Rushil's Decorative HPL is made in sheets up to 1300 × 3050 mm; the JUMBO formats run to 1830 × 4330 mm, which reduces joints on long walls and counter fronts. Confirm the format for the product and thickness you specify.
