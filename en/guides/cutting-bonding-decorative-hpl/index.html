Cutting and bonding thin decorative HPL: a workshop guide from sheet to finished edge

A bonded laminate face can finish a whole run of furniture beautifully. Whether it stays flat and clean for years is decided in the workshop: by the climate, the backing, the glue line and the last pass of the router.

6 min read·Updated Nov 26, 2026

In short

  • proHPL, the German HPL industry group, asks for HPL and substrate to be conditioned together at about 18 to 25 °C and 50 to 65 % relative humidity, stacked as they will be bonded for at least three days.
  • For sawing it recommends a feed per tooth of 0.03 to 0.06 mm with carbide or diamond tooling; for bonding, a symmetrical build-up with both faces in the same direction, and press temperatures of about 20 °C, never above 60 °C.
  • Rushil's Decorative HPL comes from 0.5 to 1.8 mm, in sheets up to 1300 × 3050 mm, and resists more than 400 revolutions to initial wear where 150 are required. These are test results, not a declaration of conformity.

How this guide was put together

The workshop figures in this guide come from the general processing recommendations of proHPL, the German HPL industry group, in their January 2022 edition. EN 438-3 defines the thin laminates intended for bonding, the ICDLI's December 2017 leaflet describes how they are used, and Rushil's Decorative HPL data sheet gives the sheet sizes and test results.

Condition the laminate and the board together

HPL and wood-based boards both take up and give off moisture, and they do it at different rates. proHPL therefore asks for the laminate and the substrate to be conditioned together before processing, in a normal room climate of about 18 to 25 °C and 50 to 65 % relative humidity. Material processed too damp tends to shrink later, which can lead to cracks and warping; material processed too dry can expand.

In practice that means air circulating around every sheet for at least ten days, and the laminate and board stacked together, the way they will be bonded, for at least three days. A pallet of cold sheets straight from the delivery truck and a stack of boards already at workshop temperature are not ready to be glued together, however good the press.

Saw with support and the right feed

The melamine surface of HPL is hard, so it wears tools faster than most woods and wood-based boards. proHPL recommends carbide-tipped tools, with diamond-tipped edges for series production. Support the thin sheet close to the cut, including the offcut, so that a narrow strip cannot flutter against the blade, and trial the cut on scrap in the same orientation as the production sheet.

For circular saws proHPL gives a feed per tooth of 0.03 to 0.06 mm. That is the distance each tooth advances, not a feed speed in metres per minute; the machine setting follows from the blade diameter, number of teeth and spindle speed. The blade projection also matters: proHPL ties the cleanest edges to a height set for the laminate thickness and the blade in use, and with hand saws the cut is made from the reverse to keep the visible edge free of breakout.

Bond a balanced panel

Two different materials glued together always pull on each other. proHPL's answer is symmetry: the best results come from a symmetrical build-up, with HPL of the same running direction on the front and the back, because laminate moves much more across its length than along it. Where the reverse is not seen, the special balancing laminates offered by HPL manufacturers do the same job.

For the glue line itself, follow the adhesive maker's instructions and keep the spread of water-based adhesives as low as practical. Stress-free panels are most reliably made at press temperatures of about 20 °C; heat shortens the press time, but proHPL advises not to exceed 60 °C, because above that the laminate and board start to move differently and the surface can suffer.

Trim the edge and round every corner

A hand router is the usual tool for trimming the overhang flush. proHPL recommends one- or two-flute carbide cutters of about 10 to 25 mm diameter at around 20,000 rpm, an overhang of only 2 to 3 mm, and a base plate covered with a non-abrasive material so it cannot mark the decor. Chips and dust must be cleared away as you go, and the sharp edge is broken afterwards.

Cut-outs need the same care. In HPL elements and compact alike, proHPL asks for every internal corner to be rounded, with a radius as large as possible and at least 6 mm. proHPL warns that notches left by poor machining can lead to cracks in HPL elements. Mark the radii on the drawing, check that the fitting accepts them, and protect any exposed wood-based substrate around openings where water may reach it.

Approve the corner the client will see

Put a finished corner and a representative opening next to the approved decor swatch, and look at them from the real viewing angle and in the project lighting. Where a run of fronts follows the grain, label the direction on the cutting list before the sheets are nested, so the pattern runs as agreed and there is still stock for every edge.

Record the finished thickness of the whole panel, not just the laminate. Handles, hinges and adjoining parts fit the assembly, so a change of substrate or backing changes the job even when the decor stays the same. If a substitute is proposed, check the face, the board, the reverse and the edge together, on a sample made with the actual construction.

Common questions

How do I saw thin HPL without chipping the face?+−

Support the sheet and the offcut close to the cut, use sharp carbide or diamond tooling and trial the orientation on scrap. proHPL recommends a feed per tooth of 0.03 to 0.06 mm for HPL.

Why must HPL and the board be conditioned together?+−

Because both take up moisture at different rates. Bonded at different moisture levels, the panel can warp or crack later. proHPL recommends about 18 to 25 °C and 50 to 65 % relative humidity, with the materials stacked together for at least three days.

Do I need a backing laminate on the reverse?+−

For a panel that has to stay flat, yes. proHPL recommends a symmetrical build-up with both faces in the same running direction, or a balancing laminate supplied by the HPL manufacturer.

What radius should an internal corner of a cut-out have?+−

As large as possible, and at least 6 mm, according to proHPL. Notches and sharp corners left by machining can lead to cracks.
