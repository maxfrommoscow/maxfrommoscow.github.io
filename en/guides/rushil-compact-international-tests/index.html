One panel, many rulebooks: the international tests behind Rushil compact laminate

Rushil Decor sells in more than 57 countries, and a panel that crosses borders meets a different rulebook at each one. The company has had its compact laminates tested to European, American, British and Japanese methods — and publishes the reports.

7 min read·Updated Oct 21, 2026

In short

  • Rushil sells its VIR brand in more than 57 countries and has its compact laminate tested to European, American, British and Japanese methods.
  • The exterior compact reaches Class A to ASTM E84 (flame spread 20, smoke 80), Class 1 to BS 476 Part 7 and B-s1,d0 to EN 13501-1, and Rushil is certified to NSF/ANSI 35 for food-service laminates.
  • VAULT emits 0.010 mg/m³ of formaldehyde in the EN 16516 test and reduces bacteria and viruses by more than 99.99 %. Each result belongs to the product and grade tested, so ask for the matching report.

How this review was put together

This article draws on Rushil Decor's published data sheets for KLADS, TOPAZ and VAULT and its compliance summaries for fire, food contact and emissions; on the ASTM E84 report issued by Intertek and the NSF certificate that Rushil publishes on its VIR website; and on the company's investor presentation of August 2025 for its markets. Each result is given with the test method and the limit it was judged against. The sources are listed at the end.

A supplier for 57 countries

Rushil Decor's August 2025 investor presentation describes distribution networks for its VIR brand in more than 57 countries — Europe, North America, the Gulf and Middle East, Saudi Arabia, Russia, Africa, Indonesia and Latin America among them — and a 'Three Star Export House' recognition. Its new jumbo-laminate plant in Gandhinagar, Gujarat, is aimed exclusively at export markets, mainly the United States and Europe.

Selling into that many markets means answering to many rulebooks at once. The tests below are the ones Rushil has published.

Europe: CE marking, EN 13501 and indoor air

KLADS EDF exterior compact is CE-marked under the Construction Products Regulation and classified B-s1,d0 to EN 13501-1, the classification set out in our review of Rushil's test margins. For interiors, the VAULT data sheet reports a formaldehyde emission of 0.010 mg/m³ in an EN 16516 chamber test — against 0.124 mg/m³ for the old E1 class, and well below the 0.062 mg/m³ that the EU has applied to furniture and wood-based articles since August 2026, which our guide to the formaldehyde limit explains; the EU limit has its own test conditions, so the comparison is indicative.

For food contact, TOPAZ was tested against Regulation (EU) No 10/2011: overall migration of 9.06, 3.07 and not detected in three successive tests against a limit of 10 mg/dm². In the SGS specific-migration tests, melamine was not detected in any of three successive migrations, against a limit of 2.5 mg/kg, and formaldehyde measured 5.31 and 4.29 mg/kg in the first two and was not detected in the third, against 15; the TOPAZ data sheet lists bisphenol A, phenol, phthalates and primary aromatic amines as not detected. Odour and taste scored 0 against a permitted 2.5.

United States: Class A, NSF and FDA

Intertek tested Rushil's exterior cladding to ASTM E84 at its laboratory in Elmendorf, Texas, in August 2024. The report gives a flame spread index of 20 and a smoke developed index of 80. The three US model codes — the International Building Code, NFPA 101 and NFPA 5000 — place interior wall and ceiling finishes with 0–25 and 0–450 in Class A, their highest class. In the UL 94 vertical burning test the KLADS data sheet reports the top rating, V-0, with no after-flame and no burning drops.

For kitchens and food service, NSF has certified Rushil Decor as complying with NSF/ANSI 35, the standard for laminates used on food-service work surfaces (certificate C0858008-01, March 2026). TOPAZ was also tested to US FDA rules 21 CFR 175.300 and 177.1460: no extractives were detected in any of the six test conditions. Rushil's compliance summaries add that its compact meets NEMA LD 3, the American laminate standard, and passes the SEFA 3 chemical stain test used for laboratory furniture, with most results at level 0 (no effect) or 1 (excellent).

California sets one of the strictest methods for indoor emissions. In the CDPH Standard Method v1.2 test, VAULT showed formaldehyde of 2–6.4 µg/m³ against a limit of 9 and total volatile organic compounds below 3 µg/m³ against 500.

Britain: BS 476

In the British BS 476 Part 7 test, KLADS EDF reached Class 1 surface spread of flame with no measurable spread — 0 mm against a Class 1 limit of 165 mm. In BS 476 Part 6 its fire propagation index was 11.6, with sub-indices of 2.3, 3.5 and 5.8.

Hygiene: Japanese and international methods

VAULT and TOPAZ have been tested to JIS Z 2801, the Japanese method for antibacterial surfaces, against eight organisms including MRSA, E. coli, Salmonella and Pseudomonas aeruginosa: reduction of more than 99.99 %. In the ISO 21702 antiviral test the reduction was 99.26 % after two hours and more than 99.99 % after 24 hours, and in ASTM G21 the surface resisted fungal growth with a rating of 0. Our guide to the hygiene and food-contact test reports explains what such figures can and cannot claim.

What this means for an export project

For a project abroad the question is not only whether a panel complies, but with which rulebook. Rushil's documents answer for several at once — and give the figures, not just the verdicts.

Each result belongs to the product, grade and thickness that was tested, so ask for the report that matches your order: the ASTM E84 report covers Rushil's exterior cladding, the CDPH and antiviral results are given for VAULT, and the food-contact results for TOPAZ.

Common questions

Is Rushil compact laminate NSF certified?+−

Yes. NSF recognises Rushil Decor as complying with NSF/ANSI 35, the standard for laminates on food-service work surfaces (certificate C0858008-01, issued March 2026).

What US fire class does Rushil's exterior compact reach?+−

Class A: in Intertek's ASTM E84 test of August 2024 it showed a flame spread index of 20 and a smoke developed index of 80, against Class A limits of 0–25 and 0–450.

Is Rushil compact a low-emitting material?+−

VAULT's data sheet reports formaldehyde of 0.010 mg/m³ in the EN 16516 test and of 2–6.4 µg/m³ in California's CDPH v1.2 method, against a CDPH limit of 9, with total VOCs below 3 µg/m³.
