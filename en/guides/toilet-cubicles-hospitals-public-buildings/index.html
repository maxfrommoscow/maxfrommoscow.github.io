Toilet cubicles and staff lockers for hospitals and public buildings: what UK guidance asks, and how compact answers

A washroom in a hospital, a school or a terminal is judged on three things: that everyone can use it, that it can be cleaned properly and that it still looks right after years of daily use. England's new toilet rules and the NHS design notes spell out the first two in unusual detail. Here is what they ask of cubicles, doors, panels and lockers, and how VAULT compact answers.

7 min read·Updated Nov 19, 2026

In short

  • England's Approved Document T, in force since October 2024, sets four toilet types: two fully enclosed rooms with full-height doors and floor-to-ceiling partitions, and two cubicles of at least 910 × 1740 mm, or 920 × 1465 mm for ambulant users, with 650 or 750 mm doors.
  • Every door must be openable from outside if someone collapses inside, preferably outward with a horizontal closing bar. NHS guidance adds that sanitary panels be easy to clean, durable and impervious, and that access panels be hinged and lockable.
  • VAULT compact answers with a 0.4 % mass gain in boiling water, grade 5 staining, more than 99.99 % bacterial and viral reduction and sheets up to 4330 mm long, enough for a floor-to-ceiling partition in one piece. These are test results, not a declaration of conformity.

How this guide was put together

This guide reads England's Approved Document T on toilet accommodation, 2024 edition with its 2025 amendments; the toilet sections of Approved Document M, Volume 2; Health Building Note 00-10 Part C on sanitary assemblies; and Health Building Note 00-02 on sanitary spaces, as published with NHSScotland's status note. It sets Rushil's VAULT data sheet against them. The documents are written for the UK and are widely read as a reference beyond it; meeting them is a matter for each project and its building control body, and this article makes no compliance claim.

Four toilet types, two of them cubicles

Approved Document T applies to new non-domestic buildings and changes of use from 1 October 2024. It defines four types. Types A and B are fully enclosed, self-contained toilets with their own washbasin, a full-height door and full-height, floor-to-ceiling partitions. Types C and D are cubicles, which the document defines as a ventilated compartment usually formed by the assembly of partition panels within a room.

The minimum sizes are precise. A Type D cubicle is at least 910 × 1740 mm with a 650 mm clear door opening. A Type C cubicle for ambulant users is at least 920 × 1465 mm with a 750 mm door, a horizontal closing bar on the inside of the door and grab rails on both side walls. In every type, a 465 mm column of clearance in front of the pan must stay clear of the door swing.

Doors that open in an emergency

The common provisions apply to every type. Doors should preferably open outward, with a horizontal closing bar. Every door must be capable of being opened from the outside if a person has collapsed against it inside, and an inward-opening door needs an emergency release so that it can be opened outward. Doors should be as light as possible, locks operable with a closed fist, and the door furniture should contrast visually with the door. Approved Document M asks the same of cubicles for ambulant disabled people.

Compact makes these details straightforward. The panel is the same material right through, so hinges, locks and closing bars screw into solid laminate rather than into a board behind a facing; VAULT's screw pullout is 3 514 N to ISO 13894-1. With 445 decors on this site, a contrasting door, frame or handle is a choice of colour, not an extra product.

Full-height partitions from one sheet

Types A and B need full-height doors and floor-to-ceiling partitions, and toilet rooms should adequately resist the passage of sound. That is where sheet size matters. VAULT is made in 1830 × 1830, 1830 × 2165, 1830 × 3660 and 1830 × 4330 mm, so a partition of normal ceiling height can be cut from a single sheet with no horizontal joint to clean around.

Thickness follows the hardware: VAULT is documented at 10, 12, 13, 15 and 18 mm, and the hinge and bracket system decides which one fits. Where the fire strategy sets a reaction-to-fire class for partitions, specify the CGF grade, which Rushil's documentation classifies B-s1,d0, and confirm the tested thickness in the classification report.

Hospitals: panels that can be cleaned and opened

Health Building Note 00-10 asks that all finishes in healthcare buildings be chosen with cleaning in mind, smooth, non-porous and water-resistant wherever blood or body fluids may be present. For the pre-plumbed assemblies that carry basins, WCs and their pipework, it asks for panels that are easy to clean, durable and impervious, and for access panels that are hinged wherever possible, with a simple lock against unauthorised opening.

VAULT's data sheet answers point by point. After immersion in boiling water the panel gains 0.4 % in mass, against 2 % allowed. It is graded 5, no visible change, in all three EN 438 staining groups, scores 0, no growth, in the ASTM G21 fungal test, and reduces bacteria and viruses by more than 99.99 % to JIS Z 2801 and ISO 21702. A solid panel needs no edge banding to open up, and a hinged access panel cut from the same sheet matches the wall around it.

Staff changing rooms: lockers in numbers

Health Building Note 00-02 asks for full-length lockable lockers in staff changing areas. For a shift system it recommends twice as many lockers as the largest number of staff on duty at one time, plus an allowance for visiting staff. As a guide to space it gives 1.1 m² per full-height locker for up to 30 lockers with changing between them, 0.75 m² beyond 30, and 1.4 m² per locker for a briefing schedule that includes changing rooms and showers.

VAULT's locker range runs from one to six tiers, every model 300 mm wide and 1830 mm high, delivered flat-packed. In the same material and decor as the cubicles, lockers, benches and partitions read as one fit-out and are cleaned the same way.

What to put in the specification

A short list keeps the tender comparable and the building control conversation short.

  • Toilet type and minimum size under Approved Document T for each cubicle or room.
  • Door swing, closing bar and emergency release; contrasting door furniture.
  • Full-height doors and floor-to-ceiling partitions where toilets are self-contained.
  • Panel thickness to suit the hardware; CGF grade where a fire class is required.
  • Hinged, lockable access panels for pre-plumbed assemblies, agreed with the infection prevention team.
  • Locker numbers and space from the staffing pattern; samples of the chosen decors.

Common questions

What size is a toilet cubicle under Approved Document T?+−

A standard Type D cubicle is at least 910 × 1740 mm with a 650 mm clear door opening; an ambulant Type C cubicle is at least 920 × 1465 mm with a 750 mm door, a closing bar and grab rails. The fully enclosed Types A and B are larger rooms with their own washbasin.

Which way should a toilet cubicle door open?+−

Preferably outward, with a horizontal closing bar on the inside. Every door must also be capable of being opened from outside if someone has collapsed inside; an inward-opening door therefore needs an emergency release, and its swing must not cross the space in front of the pan.

Can compact HPL be used for floor-to-ceiling toilet partitions?+−

Yes. VAULT is made in sheets up to 1830 × 4330 mm, so a partition of normal ceiling height comes from one sheet without a horizontal joint. Choose the thickness to suit the fixing system, and the CGF grade where a fire class is required.

How many lockers does a hospital staff changing room need?+−

Health Building Note 00-02 recommends, for a shift system, twice the largest number of staff on duty at one time plus visiting staff, and gives 1.1 m² per full-height locker for up to 30 lockers, 0.75 m² beyond, as a guide to space.
