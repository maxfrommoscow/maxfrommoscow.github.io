Ecodesign for furniture: what the ESPR will ask of furniture makers and their suppliers

The EU has ranked furniture second among the new final products in its first ecodesign working plan. No furniture rules exist yet, but the framework, the timetable and Italy's head start show what makers and their panel suppliers should get ready.

7 min read·Updated Oct 20, 2026

In short

  • Furniture ranks second among the new final products in the EU's 2025–2030 ecodesign working plan, with 2028 as the indicative year for adopting its rules.
  • The requirements can apply no earlier than 18 months after the act enters into force, and a digital product passport will be needed only if the act sets information requirements.
  • Panels are affected through the furniture rules. Rushil already provides the data makers will need: EN 15804+A2 EPDs, FSC and PEFC chain of custody, and emission tests to EN 16516 and CDPH.

How this review was put together

This article draws on the text of the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (EU) 2024/1781; on the Commission's working plan for 2025–2030; on Infobuild's report on the Italian furniture industry's new recycling consortium; on Ambiente Cucina's report of a CATAS workshop on ecodesign and product safety; and on CATAS's June 2026 FAQ on life-cycle assessment. Each point is attributed, and the sources are listed at the end.

A framework, not yet a rulebook

The ESPR, adopted on 13 June 2024, sets no product rules of its own. Requirements are laid down product group by product group in delegated acts, and they can be performance requirements, information requirements, or both. The Regulation lists the product aspects they may address:

  • Durability, reliability, reusability, upgradability and repairability; maintenance and refurbishment.
  • The presence of substances of concern.
  • Resource use and efficiency, recycled content, remanufacturing, recyclability and the recovery of materials.
  • Environmental impacts, including the carbon and environmental footprint, and the expected generation of waste.

Where furniture sits in the plan

The Regulation itself named furniture, including mattresses, among the first priorities. The working plan published on 16 April 2025 confirmed it: of four new final products, furniture ranks second in the Joint Research Centre's assessment, with an EU market of €140 billion in 2021 and an indicative adoption date of 2028. The plan expects the main gains in resource use, noting that the production and supply of materials is often the largest contributor to impacts such as climate change, acidification and eutrophication, and in waste generation. Mattresses follow in 2029.

Once a delegated act is adopted, its requirements may not apply earlier than 18 months after it enters into force, except in duly justified cases. The plan foresees a mid-term review in 2028.

Panels are an intermediate product

The working plan treats intermediate products separately because regulating them affects not only their manufacture but every final product made from them. The two intermediate products in this first plan are iron and steel, and aluminium. Wood-based panels and laminates are not on the list, so for panel suppliers the furniture act will matter through the requirements it places on furniture makers — and through the information those makers will need from their suppliers.

The digital product passport

Where an act sets information requirements, a product may be placed on the market only if a digital product passport is available, with accurate, complete and up-to-date data. The delegated act decides which data it holds, which data carrier is used, whether the passport is kept per model, batch or item, how customers can see it before buying — online sales included — and who has access to which data. The working plan expects product information to be delivered mainly through the passport.

Italy's head start

Infobuild reported in April 2025 that 26 FederlegnoArredo companies, with a combined turnover above €2.5 billion, had founded the Consorzio Nazionale Sistema Arredo (CNSA) and signed a programme agreement with the Ministry of the Environment at the Salone del Mobile. It opens a test period ahead of a possible extended producer responsibility scheme for furniture. Its aims are reuse and recycling, and design that makes furniture easier to repair, regenerate and disassemble.

The first phase samples how furniture waste is handled today in four areas — Milan, Treviso, Naples and Bari; the second consults national associations, from municipalities to suppliers of raw materials and semi-finished products, manufacturers, distributors and waste managers. CATAS, for its part, ran a workshop for furniture companies in November 2024 on the ESPR and on the General Product Safety Regulation (EU) 2023/988, as Ambiente Cucina reported.

What to prepare now

CATAS's June 2026 FAQ explains how a life-cycle assessment supports ecodesign: it shows which phases and components carry the largest impacts, and lets designers compare alternative materials at equal performance — though, CATAS adds, the best choice cannot always be known in advance and has to be tested and justified with the results.

In practice that means knowing the composition of every panel in a product, collecting the EPDs and emission test reports of the materials bought, and noting how a product comes apart — the same aspects the ESPR and the Italian consortium name.

Reading this against the Rushil range

Rushil publishes Environmental Product Declarations to EN 15804+A2 for thin and compact HPL, holds FSC and PEFC chain-of-custody certification, and reports formaldehyde and VOC emission tests to EN 16516 and CDPH Standard Method v1.2 — the kind of data a furniture maker will draw on. No furniture ecodesign requirements exist yet, so no product can be described as compliant with them today.

Common questions

When will ecodesign rules for furniture apply?+−

The 2025–2030 working plan gives 2028 as the indicative year for adopting the furniture act; its requirements can apply no earlier than 18 months after it enters into force, except in duly justified cases.

Will every piece of furniture need a digital product passport?+−

Only if the furniture act sets information requirements that call for one. The act will specify the data, the data carrier and whether the passport is kept per model, batch or item.

Are laminate panels regulated directly?+−

Not in the first working plan, whose intermediate products are iron and steel, and aluminium. Panels are affected through the requirements set for furniture.
