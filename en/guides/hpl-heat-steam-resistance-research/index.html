HPL and kitchen heat: what dry-heat, steam and burn tests measured

A kitchen or bar counter meets hot dishes, steam and the occasional cigarette. Laboratory studies from Türkiye and Portugal have put high-pressure laminate through the standard heat tests, and the melamine surface comes out well.

6 min read·Updated Oct 22, 2026

In short

  • A standard HPL surface took a container of fat at 180 °C for 20 minutes, and an hour of steam, with no visible change in Turkish Standards Institution tests.
  • An aluminium oxide overlay raised cigarette-burn resistance from grade 2 to grade 5 and multiplied wear resistance about 35 times.
  • Rushil's TOPAZ reports grade 5 for dry heat at 160 °C and for water vapour, and a 0.4 % mass gain in boiling water against a limit of 2 %. A trivet is still good practice for a pan straight off the hob.

How this review was put together

This article summarises three peer-reviewed laboratory studies: a comparison of HPL layer structures from Gazi University and Karadeniz Technical University, tested in the laboratories of the Turkish Standards Institution (Burdurlu and Özgenç, Forest Products Journal, 2009); a study of antimicrobial HPL from the University of Aveiro in Portugal (Magina et al., Materials, 2016); and a study of how the melamine resin's chemistry shapes the finished laminate, from researchers at the University of Porto (Santos et al., Journal of Mechanical Engineering Science, 2020). All three tested laboratory samples. The papers are listed at the end.

180 °C for 20 minutes, no visible change

Burdurlu and Özgenç pressed five laminate constructions and put them through the heat tests of EN 438. For dry heat, an aluminium container of fat heated to 180 °C stood on each surface for 20 minutes. The standard laminate scored grade 5, the top of the scale, meaning no visible change; so did the laminate with a barrier and an overlay, and the one with a barrier and an aluminium oxide overlay. In a one-hour steam test, the standard and the aluminium oxide constructions again scored grade 5.

The melamine surface of an ordinary HPL, in other words, already takes a hot pot and a cloud of steam without a mark. Not every added layer helped: a barrier layer on its own dropped to grade 3, which is why heat resistance is read from a laminate's test result rather than from its layer list.

Where the aluminium oxide overlay pays off

A burning cigarette is a harsher test than a hot pot, because the heat is concentrated and the ash smoulders on the surface. In the same study a cigarette was left to burn down on each laminate. The standard construction scored grade 2. With a barrier and an aluminium oxide overlay the result rose to grade 5, no visible change. That construction also scored the top grade for ink stains, against 2 for the standard laminate.

For bars, terraces and hotel counters, where a forgotten cigarette or a hot ember is a real risk, the overlay is what makes the difference. The same layer also raised the laminate's wear resistance about 35 times, as a separate article on this site explains.

A new function, heat resistance kept

Researchers at the University of Aveiro added an antimicrobial agent, polyhexamethylene biguanide, to the melamine resin of an HPL surface and checked that the laminate still did its everyday job. At 2.4 % of the resin it rated grade 4 in the 180 °C dry-heat test, meeting the required grade 4, and grade 5 for water vapour against a required 4. It also met the requirements for boiling water, impact and staining. Only scratch resistance slipped, to grade 3 against the required 4.

The point for a buyer is that heat resistance is a stable property of the melamine surface. A functional additive designed for hygiene left it intact.

Heat resistance is made in the press

Melamine-formaldehyde is the most common resin for HPL because of its durability, good fire and heat resistance and good moisture resistance, the Porto team notes. Their study shows that how far the resin is condensed before use changes its pot life, the temperatures at which it reacts and how densely it cross-links, and with them the surface quality of the finished laminate. Heat resistance is therefore built in the press, by a well-controlled resin and cure, which is why laminates are tested and classified to EN 438 rather than judged by appearance.

Reading heat on a data sheet

In the EN 438-2 dry-heat test a container of hot fat stands on the surface for 20 minutes, and the result is graded from 1 to 5, where 5 means no visible change. Rushil's TOPAZ data sheet reports grade 5 at 160 °C, against a required grade 3 for gloss and 4 for other finishes; grade 5 for water vapour; a mass gain of 0.4 % and a thickness gain of 0.2 % after boiling water, against a limit of 2 %; and a dimensional change at elevated temperature of 0.10 %, against 0.30 %. For Decorative HPL the data sheet reports grade 5 for wet heat at 100 °C. These are test results, not a declaration of conformity.

A pan taken straight from the hob can be hotter than either test, so a trivet remains good practice on any worktop.

What this means for kitchen and hospitality counters

The studies agree on the essentials. A standard HPL surface took 180 °C for 20 minutes and an hour of steam without visible change. An aluminium oxide overlay added full resistance to cigarette burns on top of its gains in wear and staining. And a surface given a new hygienic function kept its heat resistance.

For worktops, bar tops and serving counters, TOPAZ brings that melamine surface on a solid compact core, with grade 5 for dry heat and water vapour and a boiling-water gain far inside the limit. Samples and data sheets are available on request.

Common questions

Can you put hot cookware on an HPL worktop?+−

HPL is tested for it: in the EN 438-2 dry-heat test a container of hot fat stands on the surface for 20 minutes. A standard HPL showed no visible change at 180 °C in the Turkish Standards Institution tests, and Rushil's TOPAZ data sheet reports grade 5 at 160 °C. A pan straight off the hob can be hotter than either test, so a trivet remains good practice.

Does an aluminium oxide overlay improve heat resistance?+−

In the Burdurlu and Özgenç tests it kept the top grade for dry heat and steam, and raised resistance to cigarette burns from grade 2 to grade 5, while also multiplying wear resistance about 35 times.
