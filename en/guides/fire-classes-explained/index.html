Fire classes A1 to F explained: A2-s1,d0, D-s2,d0, Bfl-s1 and their national names

Every construction product in Europe is classed the same way: a letter for its part in a fire, an s for smoke and a d for burning droplets. Here is what each part measures, how floors differ, why a class is not a number of minutes, and what Polish, German, French and British rules call the same performance.

8 min read·Updated Nov 5, 2026

In short

  • Euroclasses run from A1, non-combustible, to F. The letter measures how much a product feeds a fire, s1 to s3 its smoke and d0 to d2 its flaming droplets — so D-s2,d0 is a combustible product with medium smoke and no flaming droplets.
  • Floors have their own classes with an fl suffix and no droplet class: Bfl-s1 needs a critical heat flux of at least 8.0 kW/m². And a class is never a number of minutes — minutes belong to the fire resistance of a wall or a door, such as EI 30.
  • Rushil's fire-retardant compact is classified B-s1,d0, the best class a laminate reaches. National rules read that class as niezapalny and niekapiący in Poland, schwerentflammbar in Germany and M1 in France.

How this guide was put together

This guide is based on the classification criteria in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2016/364, which sets out the European classes for the reaction to fire of construction products, and on Commission Decision 2006/213/EC for timber cladding; on the classification chart of CNBOP-PIB, Poland's national research institute for fire protection; on bauforumstahl's working aid on Germany's model administrative regulation for technical building rules, MVV TB; on the French correspondence table as reproduced in an Allianz France prevention sheet; on the UK housing ministry's impact assessment for removing the national classes; and on Rushil's data sheets.

Seven classes, four tests

EN 13501-1 sorts wall, ceiling and most other construction products into seven classes: A1, A2, B, C, D, E and F. The letter describes how much the product contributes to a fire, and every class is defined by test results, not by the name of a material.

A1 and A2 are proved with the non-combustibility test, EN ISO 1182, and the heat-of-combustion test, EN ISO 1716. Classes B, C and D — and A2 as well — also need the Single Burning Item (SBI) test, EN 13823, which measures how fast and how much heat a corner of the product releases, and the small-flame ignitability test, EN ISO 11925-2. Class E needs the small flame only, and F is what remains: a product that lets the small flame spread more than 150 mm within 20 seconds.

The limits tighten from D to B. In the SBI test the fire growth rate index, FIGRA, may be at most 750 W/s for class D, 250 W/s for C and 120 W/s for B; the total heat released in the first 600 seconds at most 15 MJ for C and 7.5 MJ for B; and for B and C the flame must not spread sideways to the edge of the specimen.

s for smoke, d for droplets

From A2 to D two more results are added. The s class is smoke, measured in the same SBI test: s1 allows a smoke growth rate, SMOGRA, of at most 30 m²/s² and total smoke in 600 seconds of at most 50 m²; s2 allows 180 m²/s² and 200 m²; s3 is anything beyond.

The d class is flaming droplets or particles: d0 means none within 600 seconds of the SBI test; d1 means none that keep burning for longer than 10 seconds; d2 is everything else, and a product whose droplets ignite the filter paper in the small-flame test is d2 as well.

Read together, D-s2,d0 is a combustible product with a fire growth rate of up to 750 W/s, medium smoke and no flaming droplets — the class an EU decision gives solid-wood cladding of at least 390 kg/m³ mounted on battens, without further testing. A2-s1,d0 is limited combustibility with the lowest smoke and no droplets; B-s3,d2 is a class B product whose smoke and droplets are not limited at all.

Floors: Bfl-s1, and no d

Floor coverings have their own classes, A1fl to Ffl, because a fire attacks a floor from above, with radiant heat. Classes Bfl, Cfl and Dfl are measured with a radiant panel, EN ISO 9239-1, which finds the critical heat flux — the radiant heat at the point where the flame stops spreading: at least 8.0 kW/m² for Bfl, 4.5 for Cfl and 3.0 for Dfl.

Floors carry only a smoke class — s1 for at most 750 %·min of smoke, s2 for more — and no droplet class. Bfl-s1 is a complete classification for a floor; there is no such thing as Bfl-s1,d0.

A class is not a number of minutes

Reaction to fire, the subject of EN 13501-1, describes how a material behaves as fuel in the early stage of a fire. Fire resistance, classified under EN 13501-2, describes how long a whole element — a wall, a floor, a door — keeps doing its job in a fully developed fire: R for load-bearing capacity, E for integrity and I for insulation, each followed by minutes.

So class B has no duration, and EI 30 says nothing about what a lining is made of. A German fire door once called T30 is, in European terms, EI2 30 Sa C5: 30 minutes of integrity and insulation, smoke tightness at room temperature, and self-closing. A specification for a partition or a door needs both kinds of class, each from its own test.

The same class under national names

Many national rules still use words rather than letters, and each country maps its words onto the European classes. In Poland, CNBOP-PIB's chart sets the mapping out: niepalne (non-combustible) covers A1 and A2 with d0; niezapalne (non-ignitable) covers the other A2 classes and every B class; trudno zapalne (hardly ignitable) covers every C class and D-s1; łatwo zapalne (easily ignitable) covers D-s2 and D-s3, E and F. On top of that, niekapiące (non-dripping) means A1 or a d0 class, and intensywnie dymiące (heavily smoking) an s3 class, E or F.

Germany's building law speaks of nichtbrennbar, schwerentflammbar and normalentflammbar, and MVV TB assigns European classes to each as minimum levels: nichtbrennbar needs at least A2-s1,d0; schwerentflammbar is met from class C, and from C-s1,d0 where low smoke and no burning droplets are required as well; normalentflammbar is met from class E. A higher class always meets a lower requirement. Since the 2019 revision of the model building code, the old DIN 4102 codes such as B1 have been replaced in the legal texts by these words.

France grades its requirements M0 to M4, and the correspondence table of the order of 21 November 2002 translates the European classes into them: A1 is incombustible, A2-s1,d0 meets M0, the other A2 classes and every B class with d0 or d1 meet M1, C classes with d0 or d1 meet M2, D classes with d0 or d1 meet M3, and every class except E-d2 and F meets M4.

In England the national classes, such as Class 0, rest on the British BS 476 tests. They were removed from the main body of Approved Document B in 2019, and the housing ministry's impact assessment sets out the plan to withdraw the remaining BS 476 references, so that only the European classes remain.

Where Rushil's panels sit

Rushil's fire-retardant compact grades — CGF for the TOPAZ worktop and the VAULT cubicle panels, EDF for KLADS exterior compact at 6 mm — are classified B-s1,d0 to EN 13501-1, the best class a laminate reaches. They clear it with room to spare: in the SBI test the interior grade shows a FIGRA of 26.1 W/s against 120, 2.4 MJ of total heat against 7.5 and 27.7 m² of total smoke against 50; KLADS EDF shows 16.15 W/s, 4.42 MJ and 29.11 m².

In the national terms above, B-s1,d0 is niezapalny and niekapiący in Poland, meets schwerentflammbar in Germany including the extra requirements for low smoke and no burning droplets, and meets M1 in France. KLADS EDF also reaches Class 1 surface spread of flame under BS 476 Part 7, with no measurable spread, and Class A under the American ASTM E84.

Two limits hold. The classification belongs to the fire-retardant grades at the tested thickness — the standard grades carry none — and no laminate reaches A2, so where a rule demands non-combustible or A2 cladding, HPL is not the answer. Fire reports are supplied on request with the product, grade and thickness.

Common questions

What does D-s2,d0 mean?+−

Class D: a combustible product with a fire growth rate of at most 750 W/s in the SBI test. s2: medium smoke, at most 180 m²/s² of smoke growth and 200 m² of total smoke. d0: no flaming droplets within 600 seconds. Solid-wood cladding on battens is typically D-s2,d0; in Poland that class counts as łatwo zapalny but niekapiący.

Is B-s1,d0 enough where A2-s1,d0 is required?+−

No. A2 is a limited-combustibility class proved in the non-combustibility and heat-of-combustion tests; B is combustible. A rule that asks for A2-s1,d0 or A1 — common for the external walls of tall buildings — rules out every laminate, fire-retardant HPL included.

What is B-s1,d0 called in national rules?+−

In Poland it is niezapalny and niekapiący; in Germany it meets schwerentflammbar, including the extra requirements for low smoke and no burning droplets; in France it meets M1. For CE-marked products the class itself is always stated in its European form.

Does class B mean 30 minutes of fire resistance?+−

No. A, B, C and the other letters describe reaction to fire: how a material feeds a fire. Minutes belong to fire resistance under EN 13501-2, which is given to whole elements such as walls and doors — for example EI 30.
